Direction
Type and spacing discipline from the General Intelligence Company: 13/15px interface sans at weight 500,
one display tracking value, tight display leading, generous vertical rhythm.
Layout from Notion: a centred statement, then one full-width band carrying three measured results. Nothing
in a sidebar.
Colour carries meaning. Indigo marks measured values, clay marks assumptions. One display face (Fraunces),
one interface face (Instrument Sans). No third.

Page structure
Hero, About, Stack, Work, then the footer. Four sections, in the owner's order: who he is, what he works
with, then the roles. Work used to sit directly under the hero and now closes the page, which puts the longest
table last and the two short blocks early.
The nav follows the page in the same order, About / Stack / Work, in the island, the mobile menu and the
footer. One consequence the owner may want to revisit: the hero's "See the work" button now jumps past two
sections to the bottom of the page.
There is no Contact section. The owner cut it and folded its contents into the hero: GitHub, LinkedIn and
Email sit as one segmented control beside the primary "See the work", so the page opens with the way in
rather than repeating it 4,000 pixels later. The résumé link that lived in Contact moved to the footer.
The social control is icons only: no labels, 52px segments with 1px dividers inside a single 44px pill.

Weight was flipped on request: the social group is now the FILLED control (`.socials--fill`, ink fill, paper
icons) and the CTA is the OUTLINED one (`.btn--line`, ink text).

The dark ramp is near neutral by request. Ground and hairlines were desaturated from 25-33% to 9-11% chroma at
the same lightness, so `--paper` #101114 reads as charcoal rather than blue-black; `--panel` #191a1e,
`--card` #1f2126, `--line` #292c31 and `--line-strong` #383c44 follow it. Text keeps a faint cool tint (`--ink`
#f1f2f5, `--body` #c3c9d6, `--muted` #99a1b2) so the page still reads ink-blue rather than grey, and `--data`
#a9b3ff is now the only saturated blue in dark mode. Contrast on the new ground: ink 16.9:1, body 11.4:1,
muted 7.3:1, data 9.5:1.

The group takes the PRIMARY button's fill, not a variant of it: `--ink` ground, `--paper` glyph, the same
inversion in dark mode. Its one difference from `.btn--fill` is a 2px outline in `--chip-line`.

`--chip-line` is the chip's own hue and chroma lifted one lightness step: #2f406a edging #131a2b in light,
#b2b7c8 edging #f1f2f5 in dark. Two earlier answers were rejected as "the colours don't feel like they belong",
and the reason was measurable in both cases. The crossed-over `--line-inverse` is a near-neutral grey (H220 S10%)
against a saturated navy chip (H222 S39%) in light mode, and in dark mode it is warm cream #d3d3cc (H60 S7%)
against cool white #f1f2f5 (H225 S17%): in each case the outline and the chip were drawn in different materials.
Keeping the hue and only moving lightness makes the pill read as one object with a lit edge, a keycap.

The edge is 1px, not 2px: at 2px on a 44px chip the outline outsized the thing it outlined. The border must
still differ from the fill in some way, because an ink border on an ink ground makes the outline and both
dividers vanish into the slab. The segments overlap by the full 2px
(`margin-left: -1px`), so the shared edge reads as one 1px divider rather than a doubled 2px seam, and the chip
is border-box, so the 44px height and 52px width are unchanged. Measured at 1x, the painted dividers are single
device pixels at offsets 51 and 102.

The CTA draws its rim from `--line-strong` too. Hover LIFTS the fill rather than darkening it: #232c45 in light
mode (the ink mixed 18% toward paper, a visible step that keeps the paper icon at contrast; the primary's own
#1d2740 sits too close to the ink to register), and #dfe3ea in dark. The dark hover has to move DOWN, not up: the chip is light there, and the previous #f5f6f8
measured 1.03:1 against the #f1f2f5 chip, so hovering did nothing visible. The
CTA keeps `order: -1` on a phone, so the action still comes first in the stacked layout even though it is no
longer the heaviest thing on the row. The filled group needs `outline-color: var(--paper)` for its inset
focus ring, because the site-wide ring is indigo and indigo disappears on an ink fill. Each link carries its
name in aria-label, which is what the labels were doing before. On mobile the group spans the column,
splits into three equal segments, and sits under the primary: the action comes first on a phone, then the
ways to reach him. That is done with order:-1 on the primary, so the DOM keeps the group first and only the
visual order changes.
The "Answers in a day. Résumé on request." line that sat under the buttons is gone at the owner's request; the
hero now goes straight from the buttons to the band.
Contact also happened to be the only block whose rule sat above its label rather than under it, so the page
lost its one stray divider in the process.
The hero carries the statement, two buttons, and the band under the label "Cooler parts of my experience":
three numbers, each with a unit and one sentence about what produced it.
Button weights are the owner's call: "See the work" is the filled primary in the second slot, and "Email me"
is the outlined secondary in the first. That reads as the right hierarchy for a portfolio, where the work
should be one click away, and it puts the bow on ink where its silver head reads best.
Sections are separated by air, not by rules. There is no divider between sections: 124px between one block's
last line and the next block's label on desktop, 92px on mobile, against 68px and 46px between the roles
inside Work. The ratio is what does the grouping.
Each block closes itself instead. The band ends on a rule, the work table ends on a rule, the stack table and
the facts table each end on one. A section's label then carries its own rule underneath, and content follows
immediately. So a reader sees: content, its closing rule, air, the next label, the label rule, content.
The footer does not add a rule of its own: the facts table already closes About, and a footer border would
put two lines about 20px apart at the end of the page. It uses padding instead.

Footer
One row on paper: name, role and year on the left, About / Stack / Work / Résumé / Top on the right, the icon
credit under the name, and the level scene along the bottom.
The only thing separating it from the page is a single 1px --line-strong rule above it, drawn by
::before at inset var(--pad) so it lines up with every other rule in the page rather than with the shell's
outer box. 72px of margin sits above the rule and 40px below it.
Two versions were rejected before this one: a full-bleed panel with a display-serif signature, and a footer
split into three labelled columns with hairlines between them. The owner wanted the footer distinguished from
the sections, not sectioned internally, and a rule plus air does exactly that.
Because the footer now owns the closing rule, the work table gave up its own border-bottom. Two rules 72px
apart at the same width read as a mistake.
Footer links keep the site's 44px minimum target and sit at an 18px gap on one line; on mobile the row wraps
and the links stay on a single line under the name.

Footer scene
A flat pixel level along the bottom edge: nine hills forming a rolling horizon, six bushes, five pipes of
different heights, a block group at the start of the level, one jump pit, a flag, and brick ground. No clouds.
1144x128 box, 1:1 with the footer box at the 1080 frame.
It borrows the composition of a side-scrolling level, not anyone's sprites. No character, no coins, no question
block; that block was removed on the owner's call because it is the most identifiable Nintendo asset here.
One unit, U = 6px, after the scene was zoomed out a quarter from a U = 8 build. Every element is a whole
number of U:
  pipe shaft    4U        24; mouth 5U = 30, lip 2U = 12
  pipe heights  9U / 7U / 5U / 4U / 4U     54 / 42 / 30 / 24, plus one at 11U = 66
  flagpole      1U x 11U  6 x 66
  pennant       3U tapering to 1U in 1U steps, 3U tall
  hill base     22U to 40U, 3 to 11 steps of 1U, inset 2U per side per step
  bush          4U x 3U   24 x 18
  brick         22px face with 2px mortar = 4U pitch, 9px course
Hills run from 18 to 66 tall so the horizon has peaks and dips rather than one repeated bump. Pipes come in five
heights: 66, 54, 42, 30 and 24.
The pit at x 690 to 770 breaks the floor and the seam only. carve() is applied to the floor courses and
nowhere else, so the hills keep running behind it.
The pit's interior is filled in d-hill, the same tone as the hills, so the gap reads as background seen through a
missing floor. Not paper, which reads as the page showing through and makes the level look cut in half; and not a
dark void, which reads as a solid block sitting in the floor. There is no d-void class; do not reintroduce one.
Both walls are lined the way the floor is lined, and the two lines join. The floor carries a 2px d-base lining
along its top edge; each wall carries the same 2px d-base lining down its full depth, placed on the pit edge
itself, x 690 on the left and x 768 on the right. That puts each vertical lining exactly where the horizontal
lining stops, so the corner closes as one L instead of two lines that miss each other by 4px, which is what
happened when the lining sat inside a separate wall face.
Three earlier versions were wrong in different ways: carving the pit through the hills deleted the background the
gap is meant to reveal; bare paper read as the page itself; a dark fill read as a block, not a hole.
How the strip crops: preserveAspectRatio is xMaxYMax slice, so the art is anchored to its right edge and
the left side is cropped away as the viewport narrows. A 390 wide window shows 754 to 1144: the flag, the last
two pipes and the right hand hills. This was xMin for one pass, to keep the jump pit out of phone crops; the owner
preferred the right edge, which keeps the flag in shot instead. Note that the right anchor does put 16px of the
pit on a 390 wide screen, at the very left edge.
Mortar is the floor only.
Blocks: one group, a single row of four at x 360 to 432 on row 3 (y 56), sitting between the first two pipes
rather than at the very start. No stacked blocks: a second tier was drawn and then removed on the owner's call.
Paint order matters here. Blocks are emitted after the hills, so they read as foreground; emitting them earlier
in the file put them behind the hill and they were clipped by its silhouette, which looks like a rendering bug.
Every position is a whole multiple of the block size in x and a whole number of blocks up from the floor line;
they sit side by side or stack exactly and never stagger by a fraction of a block. A 6px-step diagonal run was
drawn at one point and was wrong: it looked plausible but no such placement exists on the grid. The generator
asserts this and reports 0 off-grid.
One block in the group carries the accent: the fourth of the row, at x 414. It is the only colour in the scene
apart from the pennant, and the position was picked at random to break the grey.
Tones, solid colour-mix against --paper: d-hill 10%, d-bush 16%, d-ground 22%, d-block 24%, d-pipe 24%,
d-base 28%, d-pole 32%, d-top 42%, d-accent 55% of --data, d-accent-lo 78% for the highlighted block's border.
Pipes used to sit at 30%, within 2% of the floor's edging at 28%, and the two read as the same colour. Pipes are
24% now, lighter than the edging and darker than the paving, so they read as objects standing on the floor. The spread matters: with hills, ground
and blocks all inside 14 to 22% the variation existed but could not be seen. Hills are now the palest thing in
the strip and the floor is the darkest large surface, so the horizon recedes.
The canonical copy is assets/scene/foot-decor.svg; index.html inlines it verbatim.

Render sizes are 24, 16 and nothing between. 24px is pixel exact for a 24 grid icon; 16px is two thirds and
only survives because those glyphs are simple silhouettes.
The CTA arrow is Pxlkit's bow from the gamification pack, in its authored palette, at 24px like the rest of
the Pxlkit marks. At its native 16px the drawing read as a speck, because the art fills only 8 of the 16
rows; at 32px it overpowered the label. 24px is the pass that does neither.
The arrow carries a 1px rim: four hard drop-shadows one pixel out in each direction, no blur, in the divider
colour (var(--line-strong), #d3d3cc light). Light mode only. That is the same token as the eyebrow separator,
the section rules and the CTA's own border, so the arrow is edged in the page's line colour and not a new one. The CTA is now the outlined button, so the arrow sits on paper
where its silver head (#C0C0C0) and white tip would otherwise dissolve into the page; the ink rim draws the
sprite's edge for it. In dark mode the rim is switched off (`[data-theme="dark"] .ico--btn { filter: none }`)
because against dark paper it reads as a halo rather than an edge. Drawn into the grid instead, the rim would need an 18 wide box, since the tip
touches column 15, and that would put the art off the 1.5x scale. Licence copies in assets/icons/, usage notes in
assets/icons/ICONS.txt.

Colour and motion
Pxlkit art is used as authored, each glyph carrying its own palette as inline hex. Fixed hex cannot follow the
theme; measured against the surfaces it sits on, at-sign teal is 3.68:1 on paper and gear grey 3.00:1 on the
dark panel.
pixelarticons art uses currentColor, so it does follow the theme: --body for the employer mark, --muted for
the field icons, --ink for navigation.
Motion: i-code is spinning-gear, four frames on a 1.1s loop, step-end so nothing interpolates. Under
prefers-reduced-motion it stops with frame one visible.
Rejected: HackerNoon's Pixel Icon Library, because the npm package says MIT and the site says CC BY 4.0. A
portfolio should not carry an attribution question it cannot settle.

Cost of the pass
Semantics: the Pxlkit section labels are approximations. About is two people, Stack is a gear. Pxlkit has no briefcase or chart that reads at this size.
Legibility: Pxlkit's 16 grid at 24px is a 1.5x scale, so its label marks read softer than the Work icons,
which are all pixelarticons on an exact 24 or 16. Work is now the crispest section on the page, which is
appropriate since it carries the numbers.
File size: the Pxlkit glyphs dominate index.html, because each is a rect per colour run rather than one path.
Converting those runs to paths would cut it substantially if it ever matters.

About section
Three columns: a 180px label gutter, the prose, and a 260px profile column, 48px apart. 180 + 48 + 544 + 48 +
260 = 1080.
The facts used to sit under the prose as a 140px label / value table, which left the right third of the frame
empty at the 1080 width. They now run in that third as a stacked card: small-caps muted label over the value,
hairline between items, no rules in the gutter.
The prose column at 544px carries about 62 characters a line, which is inside the target range, so moving the
facts out cost nothing in measure.
Breakpoints: at 1080 and below the grid drops to 168px + 1fr and the facts move under the prose in the body
column, not into the gutter. At 860 and below everything stacks in one column, with the facts 24px below the

Voice
Written to match the live site at vansiriso.uk: first person, plain verbs, contractions, one idea per
sentence, no em dashes. His own lines there ("Human visualizing 1&0 for other humans", "connecting people to
products", "Part-time nerd") are direct and a little geeky, so the copy answers questions in the same
register instead of writing about itself.
Every sentence says who did the work. "The cleanup service made queries faster" became "I wrote the cleanup
service that made queries 30% faster and saved $1,200 a year in storage".
Rhetorical contrasts were cut. An old caption read "The mechanism, not the org chart"; it told you what the
drawing was not instead of what it was.
The h1 is the name now, not a thesis: "Vanouphon Sirisouk." with "Van", the "S" and the full stop in `--data`
(.hero__mark). That monogram is the only chromatic accent in the hero, and the heading still reads as a plain name
with the colour removed, so it is a signature and not decoration. The line is set in Geist Pixel (`--pixel`,
self-hosted, 16.9 KB), not Fraunces: this is the one display line on the page that uses the pixel face, and the
sections below stay serif. Pixel type has rules that serif settings break, so the h1 drops the negative tracking
and the Fraunces variation axes, and its line-height goes to 1.06 instead of 0.99.
The claim moved down into the deck: "Human visualizing 1&0 for other humans.", with the 1&0 set as inline `code` in
the same face. The chip is 1em, not shrunken: below roughly 16px the stepped glyph edges smear. Panel tone,
`--line-strong` hairline, small radius.

Every figure on the page is in the pixel face too: `.band__num` (the three stats) and `.item__metric b` (the per-role
metrics). Both drop negative tracking, pin `font-weight: 400` and set `font-synthesis: none`, because the face has no
bold and a synthesised one smears the blocks. Sizes: `--t-num` for the band, 22px for the inline metrics. 22 is a
floor, not a preference. At 18px the pixel steps resolve into a plain geometric and the numbers stop reading as
pixel at all, while 24 starts to shout over the body copy. Serif headings (h2) stay Fraunces: the pixel face is the
voice of the name and the numbers, not the page's default.
Scanned after editing: no em dashes, en dashes, curly quotes, or terms from the AI-vocabulary list, and no
sentence over 30 words outside the extracted SVG labels.

Shape: pills
Every button and badge is a pill, radius 999px: the hero badge, the hero buttons, the segmented social group,
the nav
links, the island and its controls, the mobile menu row, the skip link, and the fact chips.
The only remaining non-pill shape is the mobile dropdown, which is a surface rather than a control.
The hero badge is the one piece of hero furniture that is not plain paper: it sits on `--panel`, #f4f4f1 in
light and #202329 in dark, with the same 1px `--line-strong` hairline. The fill is what separates it from the
prose below it; the hairline alone let it read as another line of text. `--panel` was declared but unused before
this, so the badge is its first and only user. Light mode is at the pale end of the range: #ecece8 read as too dark a slab against pure white paper, and
#f8f8f6 stops reading as a fill at all. #f4f4f1 is the lightest step that still lands as a surface.

Chips and the badge carry horizontal padding of 16px and 13px so the rounded ends do not clip the text.

Navigation
A floating island, after the General Intelligence Company: fixed, 16px from the top, centred on the page,
60px tall, 12px radius, hairline border, a glass fill with a backdrop blur.
Glass, blur only: no frost. The fill is 38% paper and the backdrop filter is blur(16px) with no saturate.
Border, inset highlight and shadow carry the edge so the pane still reads as a surface. Gated behind
@supports, with the opaque card as the fallback.
The distinction that matters: frost is a milky layer that lightens whatever sits behind the bar. Blur alone
keeps the colours behind it and only softens their edges, so the bar looks like clear glass rather than
frosted plastic.
Consequence: over blank paper the bar renders white, because 38% white over white is white. The blur only
registers when something passes beneath it.
Legibility is a function of what is behind the bar. Over this page's light sections the labels sit at roughly
8:1; over a dense dark panel they would drop toward 2:1. If a dark section is ever added behind the bar,
raise the fill to 55-60% rather than raising the blur.
Layout: radius 999px on a 60px bar, 640px minimum width. The 44px ghost circle sits 8px from the top, 8px
from the bottom and 8px from the right outer edge, so it is concentric with the 30px pill cap. Padding is 7px
because the bar's 1px border sits outside it; 8px put the circle 9px in and broke the symmetry. Left padding
stays 22px for the brand.
The navigation group is pushed right by margin-left auto, closes with 24px of padding before the toggle, and
holds 44px links with 14px side padding and a 4px gap.
Mobile pill is 56px tall, full width, padding 0 12px 0 16px, same 999px radius.
The theme toggle is a 44px ghost circle: 1px border of ink at 22%, transparent fill at rest, on hover the
border goes to full ink and a 3.5% ink tint fills the circle.
On mobile the island toggle is hidden and the theme control moves inside the hamburger menu: a 48px row at
the foot of the list, above a hairline, with the moon or sun icon, the label "Dark mode", and a 34x20 switch.
Both buttons carry data-theme-toggle, and the script paints aria-pressed and the label on every one of them,
so the island button hidden on mobile still holds the correct state when the breakpoint changes.
Section scroll-margin-top is 112px and the hero top 116px.
The theme toggle's light-side icon is Pxlkit full-moon: at 24px crescent-moon's bite closes up and reads as a
letter C. full-moon reads as a disc with marks, so neither is ideal at this size.

Behaviour
Hides on scroll down past 180px, returns on scroll up or when focus enters it.
A nav click pins it for one second, otherwise the jump itself hides the bar you just used.
Every section id carries scroll-margin-top 112px, so a heading lands clear of the island.
The mobile menu drops out of the island as an opaque paper panel, not a translucent one.
The New York clock lives in the hero badge, after a hairline divider. It prints bare time because the badge
already names the city, uses tabular numerals so the digits do not jitter, and refreshes every 15 seconds from
America/New_York through Intl.DateTimeFormat, so a reader in another timezone still sees New York time.
The zone abbreviation comes from the same formatter with timeZoneName short, so it prints EST in winter and
EDT in summer without a hardcoded string. It renders at 11px with 0.06em tracking so it reads as a qualifier.
The skip link is fixed above the island at z-index 70.

Measure
Content column 1080px inside a 1144px outer frame at 1440, body 17px. The owner asked for the column to grow
twice: 900 to 1080 took the margins from 270px to 180px a side and the band cells from 300 to 360.
The text measure did not grow with it. Prose stays capped at 600px, which measures 62 to 77 characters a line
at 17px, and metric sentences stay at 60ch, about 72 characters at 16px. Raising the frame and the text column
together is what got reverted once: at 660px the prose ran about 80 characters and the page lost its
proportion. Width and measure are separate decisions, and the empty right margin inside a row is the price of
keeping 70 characters.
A band cell reads at 360px with the supporting sentence at three to four lines.
The first build ran a 1200px frame at 16px, which measured 82 to 90 characters a line.
The band's figures are 40px in Fraunces with tabular numerals, so 832,000 and 450,000 sit on the same optical
width. Cells are 300px each, which is why the supporting sentences are two to three lines.

Footer credits
The footer is text only. Two rows, no controls: row one is `.foot__main`, "Van Sirisouk, Software Engineer. 2026."
on the first line with the mail link alone beneath it, and the nav right-aligned. That sentence is one unit:
`.foot__id` carries `white-space: nowrap`, so it never breaks mid-sentence at a narrow width, and the title is
title-cased while the rest of the page still uses sentence case for the same role; row two is `.foot__meta` at 12px,
muted, with the "Made with" line on the left and the tool and icon credits on the right, so the width is used
without adding furniture. The credits close the row right-aligned against the nav above, and fall back to the
left edge once the meta block stacks on a phone. Both blocks align to the top of the row, so the single made
line sits level with the first credit line rather than sinking to the second. Horizontal: the text and the rule sit on `var(--pad)`. The level is the full footer box at every width,
`left: 0; right: 0; width: 100%`, so it bleeds `var(--pad)` past the column on each side.
  - Desktop, 861px and up: a mask does the framing, not the box width. Stops are `transparent 0` → `#000 var(--pad)`
    → `#000 calc(100% - var(--pad))` → `transparent 100%`, so the level is clear at its own edges and reaches full
    opacity exactly where the content column starts and ends. The landscape's visible weight is the column; the
    32px outside it is pure dissolve. Do not solve this by setting the box to the column width instead: the art is
    1144 wide, so a column-width box crops it and the ends read as a slice through hills and bricks.
  - 860px and below: no mask at all. The level runs to both screen edges, past the 24px text margins, and the frame
    edge is the screen edge, so there is nothing to hide.
An extra 24px inset on the words was tried and reverted: it only ever looked intentional on text, never on the rule
or the level.

The scene is `assets/scene/foot-decor.svg`, inlined in index.html, 187 rects in a 1144x128 box, panning from the
right on narrow screens. Every fill is a `color-mix` of `--ink` at a fixed percentage (classes .d-hill .d-bush
.d-block .d-ground .d-base .d-top .d-pipe .d-pole .d-accent .d-accent-lo), so it fades with the theme and stays
behind the text. A NES Super Mario palette was tried and rejected: fixed hex fills made the strip read as a bright
pasted-on band and flattened the page, because the scene stopped responding to the theme at all. Keep the tones
derived from --ink.

A second hill plane was built as `foot-decor-depth.svg` (ten hills, 12 to 36px, 1 to 5 steps, tone at 15% of --ink
which had to sit between the background hills at 10% and the bushes at 16%) and rejected live: ten crossing
silhouettes read as noise at 1x, because every crossing adds a stepped corner to parse and the page above is
already quiet. The file and its .d-hill-mid class were deleted on 2026-11-27. Nothing in the live scene changed:
index.html still inlines foot-decor.svg, the one-plane original, 187 rects.

What a retry would need, so the drawing does not start from nothing: three or four low hills at 12 to 18px rather
than ten, no crossings over the tall background peaks, depth from the silhouette crossing and never from an
outline (a 2px contour per step reads as horizontal stripes), the same 6px steps and 12px inset as the background
hills, the same ground line at y=110, and nothing above y=68. Judge at 1x, not on a zoom.

Vertical order, measured rather than eyeballed. `.foot__main` aligns on the BASELINE, not on the box top: the
nav's links are 44px targets with their text centred, so flex-start dropped "About" 12px below "Van Sirisouk" and
the two halves of the row read as unaligned. On a baseline they share one line whatever the box heights are, and
`.foot__meta` does the same so the made line and the first credit line sit level.
Rhythm, measured from the boundary rule to the text, not to the box: 37px to the first line, 36px between the
two rows, 32px from the last credit line to the level scene. The 37 comes from 24px of padding plus the line box;
48px of padding put 61px of air under the rule, which read as a gap rather than a section opening.
On a phone the nav drops to its own line, where those 44px boxes add 12px of air above their text, so the row gap
goes to 0 and the meta margin to 14px; measured gaps there are 16, 40 and 32.

Type carries the hierarchy, in two devices with one job each. Bold marks the person: `Van Sirisouk` is the only
bold text in the footer, so the name is the one thing the eye lands on. Italic marks a lead-in, the verb that
introduces a list of names ("Made with", "Built with", "Icons by"), which separates the label from the proper
nouns without capitals or a second colour. Nothing else is emphasised; a footer with four competing emphases
is worse than one with none.

The italics are real files, not synthesised: the roman woff2 files carry no italic axis, so `font-style: italic`
was shearing the roman, which is visible at 12px on a serif page. `assets/fonts/instrument-sans-italic.woff2`
is now declared and used; `fraunces-italic.woff2` is declared but unused, so the browser never requests it.

comes out of the identity line: the year stays there, and New York survives only in the made line.
