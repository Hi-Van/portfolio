The CTA arrow
The icon is Pxlkit's arrow from the gamification pack: a green bow, a brown shaft, a silver head. Van picked
it over the ui pack's plain arrow-right, and it carries its authored palette (S #C0C0C0, B #8B4513,
G #228B22, W #FFFFFF) exactly like every other Pxlkit glyph.
Size took three passes. At 16px, its native grid, it read as a speck: the drawing only occupies 8 of the 16
rows, so the visible art was around 8px tall inside a 44px button, and the #C0C0C0 head sat at 1.8:1 on
paper. At 32px the art matched the label's cap height twice over and pulled the eye off the words. 24px is
where it settles, and it is also the size every other Pxlkit mark on the page renders at, so the button now
follows the set rather than inventing a size of its own.
The rim
The button fill inverts with the theme: ink in light mode, paper in dark. On paper the silver head (#C0C0C0)
sat at 1.8:1 and the white tip disappeared, so the icon was weak in exactly half the page's life.
The fix is a 1px rim: four hard drop-shadows, one pixel in each direction, in #0a0d15, applied to .ico--btn.
No blur, so the edge stays a pixel edge rather than a glow. The colour is the same in both themes, which is
what lets one rule do both jobs. Against the ink fill the rim is the fill, so it disappears; against the
paper fill it reads as a contour and the bow separates cleanly.
Chained drop-shadows build on each other, so the rim is a little heavier than a single pass would give. At
24px that reads as a deliberate pixel outline, which is the intent.
A true pixel outline drawn into the grid was the other option and it does not fit: the arrow's tip touches
column 15 of a 16 wide icon, so there is no room for a rim on the right without shrinking the art or moving
to an 18 grid, which would put the render off the 1.5x scale the rest of the set uses.

Colour and motion
Pxlkit art is used exactly as authored: every glyph carries its own palette as inline hex, no
remapping, no opacity tricks. That is what makes the medal gold, the friends mark blue and pink, the
at-sign teal and the scroll parchment. The cost is that the art cannot follow the theme, because the
hex is fixed. Measured against the surfaces it sits on:
  scroll parchment #F5DEB3 on the light panel  1.21:1   (its outline #8B4513 carries it at 6.56:1)
  search lens #B0D4EE on the light panel       1.44:1
  package highlight #D4A04A on the light panel 2.17:1   (body #8B6914 is 4.7:1)
  at-sign teal #0891B2 on paper                3.68:1
  gear grey #556677 on the dark panel          3.00:1
So on white the pale fills read as light shapes and the dark outlines do the work. If the full palette
needs to show, the icons need a darker or tinted ground, not a new palette.

Animation
Two symbols animate, because only two of the drawings this page uses exist as multi frame icons in
Pxlkit:
  i-code  spinning-gear, four frames, 1.1s loop
  i-sun   pulsing-sun, four frames, 1.1s loop
Each frame is a <g class="pxl-f"> with animation-delay at a quarter of the cycle, and
@keyframes pxl-step-4 holds it visible for one quarter then hides it. pacing is step-end, so the
pixels never interpolate.
Under prefers-reduced-motion the animation is switched off and frame one stays visible, rather than
freezing on an empty frame.
The rest of the set is single frame art in Pxlkit's catalogue: compass, xp-bar, medal, friends, at-sign,
grid, cloud-sync, scroll, package, search, upload, crescent-moon. They are still by design, not by
omission. Pxlkit's animated twins for other concepts are bouncing-arrow, ringing-phone, blinking-eye,
floating-hearts, shaking-bell, spinning-tornado, pulsing-dot, loading-circle and sparkle-star if a
concept should be remapped to one.

Known cost of this pass
Twelve of the eighteen concepts are approximations. Pxlkit is a game and app vocabulary and has no
files, database, monitor, chart, code, briefcase, user, mail, layout, server, GitHub or LinkedIn glyph.
Work is a medal, About is two people, the catalog record is a box. The meaning still lands, but the
label carries more of it than the picture does.
At 16px the art is denser than the 24 grid glyphs it replaced, so label markers read as small coloured
marks rather than crisp shapes. That trade cannot be tuned away without redrawing at 24.

Field icons
Every role header labels its fields: the employer mark before the title, a person before the company name,
a pin before the city, a calendar before the date.
The employer mark renders at 24px, the three field icons at 16px, and all four centre in one 24px column at
the content edge, so every text run starts at the same x. The 16px icons carry 4px of margin on each side to
sit in that slot.
The employer mark is --body (class ico--id) and the three field icons are --muted, so the one mark that
changes per row still carries more weight than the labels that repeat on every row.
24px is also the size where a 24-unit icon is pixel exact. The 16px field icons run at two thirds scale, so
next to the sharp title mark they read slightly softer. That is the cost of the size hierarchy the owner
asked for; redrawing those three on a 16 grid is the fix if it ever needs to be exact.
The owner asked for the title and company icons to trade places, so the company's mark now sits on the job
title line and the person sits on the company line. That inverts the literal reading on purpose; the tone
difference is what keeps the varying mark legible as identity rather than as a label.
16px on a 24 grid is a two thirds scale, so the Work header is the one place where the pixels are not 1:1.
They still read because each shape is a silhouette with few details; anything finer would turn to mush at
this size and should be redrawn on a 16 grid instead, the way the Pxlkit set is.

The Work label was the worst case and got fixed rather than accepted. Pxlkit's medal drew as a yellow jar
with a red bow at 16 units, which read as a present beside the word Work. It is now pixelarticons'
briefcase on the 24 grid, drawn at 24px, so it is sharp and it matches the five role marks under it.
That leaves the Work section monochrome while the other section labels stay in Pxlkit colour. The trade is
deliberate: six coloured marks down one column would compete with the indigo metric figures, while a
monochrome set sits behind them.

Implementation note
Pxlkit icon data uses quoted palette keys ("P": "#F5DEB3"). A parser that only accepts bare keys gets
an empty palette and the icon renders blank. scroll and medal both did that on the first pass.

Navigation and social, kept from pixelarticons
Reason: the owner asked for those to stay, and they are the icons a reader uses to move around, so
they stay in the crisper 24 grid set rather than the denser game art.

Attribution
The footer carries "Icons by Pxlkit and Pixelarticons", each linked. Pxlkit's asset licence requires a
visible credit with a link plus the notice kept in source distributions; both are satisfied.
Pxlkit's icon packs are source-available, not open source: free to use and modify with credit, no
redistribution as an icon pack, no trademark rights. The code packages are MIT. pixelarticons is MIT.
